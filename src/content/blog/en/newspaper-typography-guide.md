# The Ultimate Guide to Newspaper Typography

Typography has always been the heart and soul of newspaper design. Every font chosen for a broadsheet conveyed urgency, authority, and clarity under intense production constraints.

---

## 1. The Headings Hierarchy

A classic newspaper layout establishes immediate hierarchy:
1. **The Banner / Screamer**: Full-page width, all-caps, heavy font (60pt–96pt).
2. **The Kicker / Eyebrow**: A short single-line category above the headline in smaller sans or italic serif.
3. **The Sub-head / Deck**: 2–3 lines summarizing the main story before jumping into column copy.

---

## 2. Choosing the Right Display Font

- **Blackletter (Gothic)**: Best for Victorian, 19th-century, and European mastheads.
- **Classic High-Contrast Serif (Playfair, Bodoni, Caslon)**: Perfect for early 20th-century broadsheets and investigative scoops.
- **Condensed Bold Sans (Bebas, Franklin Gothic)**: Ideal for 1950s–1980s sensational tabloids and breaking news editions.
- **Monospace Typewriter (Special Elite)**: Gives the raw, unedited feel of an undercover reporter's telegraph or confidential dispatch.

---

## 3. The Art of the Drop Cap

A drop cap anchors the reader’s eye directly to the beginning of the narrative. Traditional printers would set an oversized metal letter spanning two or three lines of body type, often ornamenting it with delicate flourishes.
